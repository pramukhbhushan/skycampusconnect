The meeting focused on mapping the college-course funding, registration, accounting, and closure workflow for SKY Campus, identifying the manual work and failure points, and planning a platform to guide organizers and administrators. Pramukh and Aryan discussed building an application that brings the scattered forms and follow-ups into one tracked workflow; Kelsey explained the current process and what information the team could share.
Finance and course workflow
Each campus first follows its own university procedures and deadlines. For example, Purdue requires an event request at least two weeks before the event. When a campus secures school funding, it submits a SKY Campus Google Form requesting that the Journey course be made free ($0) for students, with proof of funding. The course must already be posted on Journey so the request can include its course ID. Some schools, such as UCLA, also need a quote before requesting an invoice. The invoice number is later used in course accounting and helps connect the campus payment to the course.
Students may complete a campus Google Form and an orientation call before registering through the Journey link. The call confirms their intent and ability to attend the three-day course; Journey uses the student's EDU email to verify college-student status. After the course, the teacher or authorized person records attendance in Journey and submits the course for closure, including the invoice number where applicable. The team then tracks whether the funding institution has paid IAHV and whether the course is fully closed.
Eight steps discussed
Follow the campus's internal event, room, and funding requirements and deadlines.
Post the course on Journey and obtain its course ID.
If school funding is being used, submit the $0 request form with the course ID and proof of funding.
Submit any required quote and invoice requests. Some schools need a quote first; invoice requests use a Google Form. Earlier is preferred, though Finance had said the request can be made on the course day.
Collect student sign-ups and conduct orientation calls to confirm students understand and can commit to the three days; send the Journey registration link for EDU-email verification.
After the course, record each student's attendance status in Journey.
Enter the invoice number in the course accounting/closure process and submit the course for closure.
Track payment from the school or funder and follow up until payment and course closure are complete.
Attendance categories were clarified: attending the Friday and Saturday Sudarshan Kriya sessions counts as complete, even if the student misses the third day; attending only the first day is a dropout; signing up but not attending is a no-show. Kelsey said dropouts and no-shows still require payment, but was unsure whether a cancellation has the same payment consequence. Attendance is selected manually in Journey.
Manual work and pain points
Organizers and teachers often forget to request an invoice, submit course accounting after the course, or include the invoice number when closing the course. The invoice request form is lengthy, contributing to delays and incomplete accounting.
IAHV Finance (Mikayla) maintains a spreadsheet of open courses and payment/closure status. Kelsey described the sheet as manually maintained, though she was not certain whether some fields are copied from invoice forms.
Kelsey searches the spreadsheet for overdue courses and personally texts organizers or teachers one by one to ask them to submit accounting or close a course. Even when a course has been paid, it can remain open until someone submits the closure in Journey.
If an invoice was never requested, Finance may create a placeholder invoice number so the course can still be closed. The invoice number also connects incoming payment to the campus/course.
Invoice and payment records are somewhat confidential. Kelsey planned to check with Mikayla before sharing tracking data; a safe alternative discussed was a template with column names and monetary details removed.
Orientation steps are documented in resource pages, but Kelsey has noticed some campuses are not following them consistently. Her existing reminder email often goes out only during the week of the course, potentially too late for orientation.
Platform discussed
The proposed product is a single application that makes the steps visible and reduces the need to hunt through separate Google Forms. The group discussed a progress bar/checklist, forms grouped in one place, and a way to add or import future forms if the process changes. Completed form information could populate a master sheet rather than requiring duplicate manual entry.
The two main profiles are Organizer and IAHV Finance/admin. Organizers would enter their name, email, and campus and see their own course progress. The admin/finance view would show courses across campuses, allow sorting or filtering by campus and status, and show organizers' progress. SKY Campus administrators could use the same administrative portal rather than needing a separate third role. Access to older records and confidential fields would be limited to authorized admins.
For reminders, the initial plan was email rather than automated texting. The app could prepare a personalized email with subject and message for review and sending. A status-based queue could support individual or bulk follow-ups—for example, selecting courses with pending payment or accounting. The team also discussed eventually automating the master-sheet data flow. Hosting on Vercel was proposed, with possible open-source access for SKY Campus leaders; the builders planned to share progress for feedback.

Agreed direction
Start with two roles—Organizer and IAHV Finance/admin—with SKY Campus admins sharing the admin portal. Begin with email reminders; leave text automation for later. The central goal is to make the funding-to-closure workflow easier to follow and reduce missed invoices, incomplete course accounting, manual spreadsheet tracking, and one-by-one reminder messages.
